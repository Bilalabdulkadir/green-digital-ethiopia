# 🌱 Green Digital Ethiopia

**Green Digital Ethiopia** is an independent research and software project exploring the intersection of:

- 🤖 Artificial Intelligence
- 🌱 Green ICT
- ⚡ Energy efficiency
- 🏢 Data-centre sustainability
- ☁️ Sustainable cloud computing
- ♻️ ICT lifecycle management
- 🔄 Electronic-waste management
- 📊 Data analytics
- 🏛️ Digital policy
- 🌍 Sustainable digital transformation

The project investigates how AI, software engineering, data analysis, and sustainability frameworks can support more efficient, measurable, resilient, and environmentally responsible digital infrastructure in Ethiopia.

## 📖 About the Project

Digital transformation is creating increasing demand for:

- Computing infrastructure
- Data centres
- Cloud services
- Network expansion
- Electronic hardware deployment

While digital systems can support economic growth, public service delivery, and innovation, they also consume significant electricity, require specialized cooling solutions, generate electronic waste, and create long-term infrastructure dependencies.

This project examines how digital infrastructure in Ethiopia can be planned and operated sustainably, balancing technological ambition with environmental and resource constraints.

## 📄 Abstract

Green Digital Ethiopia is a computational research and policy-analysis platform designed to evaluate the environmental performance and strategic implications of data center expansion and digital infrastructure investment in Ethiopia. The project brings together empirical assumptions, regional benchmarks, and deterministic modeling to assess energy use, water consumption, carbon emissions, backup-generation impacts, lifecycle effects, and policy design considerations.

The platform is intended for researchers, policy analysts, and digital infrastructure planners seeking to understand how Ethiopia can build sustainable, resilient, and low-carbon digital systems. It models indicators such as Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE), Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE), annual electricity demand, emissions intensity, embodied carbon, and circularity opportunities, while also providing AI-assisted policy analysis grounded in regional sustainability constraints.

---

## 🌍 Research Motivation

The rapid growth of digital infrastructure in emerging economies raises critical questions about sustainability, infrastructure planning, resilience, and governance. In Ethiopia, where digital transformation, industrialization, and public-sector modernization are strategic priorities (notably under the **Digital Ethiopia 2025** strategy and national cloud initiatives), data center development must be evaluated not only in terms of capacity and economic value but also in relation to:

- **Energy security and grid reliability:** Balancing high-power compute clusters with national grid supply dynamics.
- **Climate and emissions implications:** Quantifying operational carbon against Ethiopia's predominantly renewable (~90%+ hydro, wind, and geothermal) grid baseline.
- **Water-resource constraints:** Evaluating cooling choices to minimize evaporative water consumption in municipal water networks.
- **Cooling technology selection:** Leveraging Addis Ababa's high altitude (2,300m above sea level) and moderate 16°C annual mean temperature for ambient free-air economization.
- **Backup power dependence:** Accounting for diesel generator runtimes during grid transmission outages and fuel emissions.
- **Circular economy and e-waste pathways:** Mitigating Scope 3 embodied manufacturing emissions by extending server hardware lifespans from 3 to 5+ years.
- **Long-term policy alignment:** Providing verifiable benchmarks and scorecards aligned with Ethiopia's Climate-Resilient Green Economy (CRGE) strategy.

Green Digital Ethiopia addresses this gap by enabling scenario-based analysis of digital infrastructure sustainability using a combined modeling, evidence review, and advisory framework.

---

## ❓ Research Questions

This platform is structured to help answer the following core research questions:

1. **Energy and Cooling Optimization:** How do data center design choices and high-altitude ambient conditions influence energy and water consumption in Ethiopia?
2. **Lifecycle Trade-offs:** What operational configurations minimize total lifecycle environmental impact across both operational (Scope 2) and embodied hardware (Scope 3) emissions?
3. **Climatic Suitability:** How do regional climate conditions across Ethiopia (e.g., Addis Ababa vs. rift valley zones) affect cooling economizer feasibility and PUE performance?
4. **Reliability vs. Carbon:** What are the quantitative trade-offs between grid dependence and diesel backup generation during grid instability?
5. **Circularity Potential:** How can circularity, extended refresh cycles, component refurbishment, and waste-heat reuse reduce the overall digital infrastructure footprint?
6. **Policy Governance:** What regulatory mechanisms, power purchase agreements (PPAs), and public procurement standards support green and resilient digital infrastructure investment?

## 💡 Core Contributions

The project contributes to research and policy practice in four principal areas:

1. **Sustainability Modeling**
   - Quantitative, deterministic analysis of PUE (ISO/IEC 30134-2:2016), WUE, fleet energy demand, operational carbon (GHG Protocol Scope 2), and hardware lifecycle metrics.
2. **Regional Evidence Integration**
   - Incorporation of empirical sectoral assumptions, regional benchmarks (IEA Africa Energy Outlook, EEP generation mix, Uptime Institute benchmarks), and evidence classifications (`measured`, `user_estimate`, `scenario_assumption`).
3. **AI-Assisted Policy Synthesis**
   - Grounded qualitative policy synthesis utilizing the Google Gemini API (`@google/genai`), operating strictly as a policy advisor on top of immutable deterministic calculations, with complete offline deterministic fallback.
4. **Decision Support for Public and Institutional Planning**
   - Interactive scenario exploration, sensitivity analysis with visual chart decomposition (Recharts), and policy brief generation for digital infrastructure planning in urban and industrial parks.

## 🏛️ System Overview

Green Digital Ethiopia is implemented as a full-stack web application that combines:

- a **React + TypeScript frontend** for interactive scenario analysis and data visualization,
- a **Vite-based development and build environment**,
- an **Express backend** providing RESTful API endpoints for health status, advisor synthesis, and Google Drive research archiving,
- **deterministic sustainability algorithms** ensuring reproducible, zero-hallucination calculations,
- and an **optional AI-powered advisor** through the Google Gen AI SDK.

## 🛠️ Features

### Scenario Analysis & ScenarioLab
Users can specify facility characteristics and operational settings:

- **IT capacity & server count**
- **Server utilization & power rating**
- **Cooling architecture:** Direct air economizer, chilled water with cooling tower, closed-loop adiabatic, or direct-to-chip liquid cooling
- **Target Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE)**
- **Geographic location & altitude baseline** (Addis Ababa, Hawassa, Dire Dawa, Bahir Dar)
- **Grid emission factor:** Baseline EEP grid (~0.032 kgCO2e/kWh) vs. regional averages
- **Dedicated clean energy PPA percentage**
- **Backup diesel generator runtime hours**
- **Hardware baseline & extended lifespans (years)**
- **Server embodied manufacturing footprint & device weight**
- **Circularity and waste heat reuse parameters**

### Recharts Visualization Breakdown
The integrated **ScenarioLab Charts** display real-time, interactive breakdowns of:
- **Energy Consumption Breakdown:** Baseline facility energy vs. optimized facility energy partitioned by IT load and cooling/overhead.
- **Operational & Avoided Carbon Breakdown:** Scope 2 grid emissions, diesel backup emissions, clean PPA abatement, and Scope 3 circularity savings.

### Evidence Review & Frameworks
- **Conceptual Models:** High-altitude cooling thermodynamics, renewable grid dynamics, circular IT lifecycles, and policy interventions.
- **Evidence Review:** Authoritative benchmarks categorized with strict epistemological tiers (`measured`, `user_estimate`, `scenario_assumption`).
- **Policy Brief Generator:** Exportable institutional briefs formatted for policymakers and infrastructure planners.

### AI Policy Advisory
The backend exposes an advisory endpoint that synthesizes policy recommendations across four focus areas:
- PUE optimization and cooling economization
- Grid integration and clean power wheeling
- Hardware lifecycle circularity and e-waste stewardship
- National regulatory policy and procurement codes

When a Gemini API key is unavailable or rate-limited, the system seamlessly uses a deterministic expert policy corpus to guarantee uninterrupted operation.

## 📐 Methodological Approach & Mathematical Formulations

All quantitative metrics are governed by deterministic formulations conforming to international standards:

### 1. Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE)
Conforms to **ISO/IEC 30134-2:2016**:
$$\text{PUE} = \frac{\text{Total Facility Energy (kWh)}}{\text{IT Equipment Energy (kWh)}} = 1 + \frac{\text{Cooling \& Infrastructure Overhead (kWh)}}{\text{IT Equipment Energy (kWh)}}$$
- **Domain Constraints:** $\text{IT Energy} > 0$, $\text{Total Facility Energy} \ge \text{IT Energy}$ ($\text{PUE} \ge 1.0$).

### 2. Fleet Efficiency Savings
$$\text{Annual Fleet Energy Saved (kWh)} = \frac{N_{\text{servers}} \times \Delta W_{\text{server}} \times H_{\text{daily}} \times 365}{1000}$$

### 3. Operational Greenhouse Gas Emissions (GHG Protocol Scope 2)
$$\text{Operational Emissions (tCO}_2\text{e)} = \frac{\text{Energy (kWh)} \times \left[(1 - f_{\text{PPA}}) \times EF_{\text{grid}} + f_{\text{PPA}} \times EF_{\text{PPA}}\right]}{1000}$$
- Backup generation:
$$\text{Diesel Emissions (tCO}_2\text{e)} = \frac{P_{\text{gen (kW)}} \times H_{\text{backup}} \times EF_{\text{diesel}}}{1000}$$

### 4. Hardware Lifecycle & Avoided E-Waste (Scope 3)
$$\Delta N_{\text{devices saved}} = N_{\text{devices}} \left( \frac{1}{L_{\text{baseline}}} - \frac{1}{L_{\text{extended}}} \right)$$
$$\text{Avoided Embodied Carbon (tCO}_2\text{e)} = \frac{\Delta N_{\text{devices saved}} \times CF_{\text{embodied}}}{1000}$$
$$\text{Avoided E-Waste (metric tons)} = \frac{\Delta N_{\text{devices saved}} \times W_{\text{device}}}{1000}$$

## 📊 Sustainability Indicators

| Category | Indicator | Definition & Unit |
|---|---|---|
| **Energy & Efficiency** | **PUE** | Total Facility Energy / IT Equipment Energy (dimensionless ratio) |
| | **Cooling Overhead** | Percentage of facility energy dedicated to cooling and auxiliary systems (%) |
| | **Annual Electricity Demand** | Total facility annual consumption (MWh / GWh) |
| | **Free-Cooling Availability** | Estimated annual compressor-free economizer hours based on altitude (hours/year) |
| **Water & Thermal** | **WUE** | Water Usage Effectiveness (L/kWh of IT energy) |
| | **Evaporative Water Footprint** | Annual cooling tower makeup water consumption ($m^3$/year) |
| | **Waste Heat Recovery** | Low-temperature thermal energy potential for district heating or agro-processing (GJ/year) |
| **Emissions & Climate** | **Scope 2 Grid Carbon** | Location-based emissions from Ethiopian Electric Power grid (tCO2e/year) |
| | **Scope 1 Backup Diesel** | Stationary combustion emissions during outage intervals (tCO2e/year) |
| | **Clean PPA Abatement** | Avoided operational emissions via dedicated renewable wheeling (tCO2e/year) |
| | **Scope 3 Embodied Carbon** | Lifecycle manufacturing carbon avoided through extended server lifespans (tCO2e/year) |
| **Circularity** | **Avoided E-Waste** | Mass of decommissioned electronic equipment diverted from informal scrap streams (kg/year) |
| | **Server Lifespan Extension** | Baseline operational cycle vs. extended refresh cycle (years) |
